# Power Monitor work-alike 3D printed console
The console prints in four parts. Each STL is oriented for FDM printing as published. 
The front panel and back panel are designed
to accommodate a filament color change at Z = 1.2mm in order to acheive a different color background for the
text labels on those panels. The text prints better with a smaller than standard nozzle diameter.
I used 0.3mm.

The sub-panel part is prone to warping while being 3D printed. A brim helps control that, although it also makes post print
processing a nuisance.

<h3>Assembly</h3>
<p>The sub-panel has a press-in hex fitting on each of its sides for a #12-28 (fine thread) nut that holds the console to
a pivoting mount. Tap them in with a hammer. (The
OEM console uses M5 hardware for its pivot mount. Why it has one bit of metric hardware with imperial
everywhere else is a mystery for the ages.) The potentiometer and the 3-way switch attach to 
their respective holes in the front panel. Each requires 3 wires to
the PCB. Disassembly will be much easier later if you use connectors at the PCB. I terminated 
those wires in a 3 pin header. The PCB has
0.1" spacing for its end. The 12V LED strip for meter back light has a self adhesive into the top channel
of the front panel. Its two wires should be terminated in a 2 pin header, spaced 0.1" as well. Five #4 square
nuts go in the four corner channels of the front panel plus one in the middle between the meters. They
should press fit and stay in place, but, if not, use a bit of cellophane tape to keep them from falling
out as the front panel is handled later.</p>
<p>The PCB fastens to the back panel with two #4 machine screws and two 3/8" #4 hex nuts. 
Also on the back panel are recesses for two #6 hex nuts that will be
used later to fasten the back panel to the sub panel.</p>


Each meter fastens to its holes in the sub panel. Each has four screw posts, but a nut on only two
of the four is plenty. With the meters, the potentiometer, and the 3-way switch LED back light, and #4 square nuts all assembled,
the front panel 
fastens to the sub panel with quantity five #4 x 3/8" screws. 

The back panel, with the PCB assembled to it and hex nuts pressed into the appropriate recesses, slides into
the sub panel. Before fastening with #6 by 5/16" screws through the bottom of the sub panel, fit the lid into 
position on the top and then the whole assembly can be held together in one had while the two screws
are tightened from the bottom. I used black phillips head #6 screws.
